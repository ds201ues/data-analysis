THE EIGHT LENSES — PASTE-READY PROMPTS
Reading Data Masterclass · Dishant Singla · run on Aurex, inside BCCL
Practice file: ad-sales-data.csv

Each prompt is written to force checkable output: it makes the tool state what
it dropped, show its working, and label what it assumed. Paste them, do not
retype them — the wording is doing the work.

==============================================================================

LENS 1 — COMPARISON
  Question it answers : How does this one stack up against that one?
  Trap it hides       : Mumbai is biggest at everything. Biggest is not best.
  Catch it by         : divide by something. Per rep, per order, per rupee. Then re-rank.

  PROMPT:
    Filter ad-sales-data.csv to status = Confirmed. Tell me how many rows you dropped, and why.
    The city column has spelling variants. Show me the mapping BEFORE you apply it.
    Total net_amount by city, sorted high to low.
    Give me the gap between top and bottom as a multiple, not as two numbers.
    Horizontal bar chart: sorted, one accent colour, values on bars, title = the finding as a sentence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 2 — RANKING
  Question it answers : Who is on top, and who is at the bottom?
  Trap it hides       : A top-10 flatters. The bottom holds the recoverable money.
  Catch it by         : print both ends, with the order count beside every name.

  PROMPT:
    Rank all 30 reps by total net_amount on confirmed bookings only.
    Show me the top 5 AND the bottom 5 in one table, with order count beside each name.
    Flag any rep with fewer than 10 orders. I do not want a rank built on three deals.
    State the gap between rank 5 and rank 25 as a multiple, and say what it would take to close it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 3 — TREND
  Question it answers : What changed over time, and when exactly?
  Trap it hides       : Raw lines let the biggest series bury every other story.
  Catch it by         : index every series to a common base before you plot anything.

  PROMPT:
    Monthly net_amount by ad_type, 2022 to 2024, confirmed bookings only.
    Index every series to its own first month = 100, so I compare shapes and not sizes.
    Name the month each series changed direction. Do not just tell me it is growing.
    Exclude any part-finished month at the end, and tell me explicitly if you excluded one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 4 — COMPOSITION
  Question it answers : What makes up the total, and is the mix moving?
  Trap it hides       : Volume can rise while revenue stalls. That is mix.
  Catch it by         : plot share and absolute together. If they disagree, mix is the story.

  PROMPT:
    Share of net_amount by ad_type for each year: 2022, 2023, 2024. Confirmed only.
    Give me share % and absolute rupees in the same table, side by side.
    Tell me which category gained share while losing rupees, or the reverse. That is the finding.
    100% stacked column by year. Label only the categories that moved more than 2 points.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 5 — DISTRIBUTION
  Question it answers : What does a typical one actually look like?
  Trap it hides       : A handful of whales drag the average away from reality.
  Catch it by         : ask for both, plus the ratio. Above 1.3× the mean is misleading you.

  PROMPT:
    Distribution of net_amount across confirmed bookings.
    Give me mean, median, p25, p75, p90 — and the mean-to-median ratio as a number.
    Histogram with the median drawn as a line on the chart, not just quoted in the caption.
    If that ratio is above 1.3, tell me in one sentence which figure I should quote, and why.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 6 — CORRELATION
  Question it answers : Do these two things move together?
  Trap it hides       : Two things moving together is not one causing the other.
  Catch it by         : name the third thing — season, campaign, pricing.

  PROMPT:
    Scatter discount_pct against net_amount for confirmed bookings.
    Give me the correlation coefficient, and say plainly whether that is strong, weak or nothing.
    Do NOT describe this as one thing causing the other. I will ask for that separately.
    List three other factors that could be driving both, given this is ad sales data.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 7 — OUTLIERS
  Question it answers : What looks wrong, and is it real or a data error?
  Trap it hides       : A few large deals can manufacture a trend.
  Catch it by         : re-run the headline with the top 1% removed. If it vanishes, say so.

  PROMPT:
    Flag every booking above 3× the median net_amount. How many, and what % of revenue are they?
    Show them in a table with client, city, rep and date, so I can eyeball them for data errors.
    Then re-run the revenue growth headline with those rows excluded. Show me both numbers.
    Tell me straight whether my headline survives without them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENS 8 — DECOMPOSITION
  Question it answers : Why did the number move?
  Trap it hides       : “It went up” is not a driver. The parts must sum.
  Catch it by         : demand the residual. Not near zero means not finished.

  PROMPT:
    Net revenue moved from ₹12.81 Cr in 2023 to ₹15.54 Cr in 2024. Split that change, with code.
    volume effect = (orders24 − orders23) × avg23    size effect = (avg24 − avg23) × orders24
    The two must sum to the total change. Print the reconciliation and print the residual.
    If the residual is not about zero, stop and tell me what is unexplained.
    Label every driver OBSERVED or INFERRED.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE FIVE-CHECK GATE — run this before you send anything

  1. Row accounting      Ask how many rows it used. Compare to the file.
  2. Spot-verify         Check the single number that matters against the source.
  3. Ask it a second way Different phrasing, same question. Two answers means none.
  4. Open what it made   Look at the axis. Read the labels.
  5. Sign it             Would you put your name on this? You are about to.
